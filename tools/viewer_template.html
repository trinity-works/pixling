How it's built

Pixel Perfect is a Python tool with no image model inside. An AI artist designs by writing specs, the forge turns them into pixels, and the artist looks at the result and iterates.

Write a spec

The artist describes a character in pixels: bones, simple shapes on each bone (ellipsoids, capsules, cones, boxes), materials from the style, and hand-placed pixel stamps for eyes and teeth.

Bake 8 directions

The hidden 3D model is ray-marched at 4× and reduced by coverage and majority vote. Nothing rotates at runtime; each of the 8 facings is a fixed, cleaned bake.

Keep pixels still

Every bone is cached as its own layer and composited at whole-pixel offsets. A part that only moves keeps exactly the same pixels, so nothing swims or shimmers.

Quantize last

Pixels are material plus light level until the very end, then snap to the style's locked ramps. Edges keep their fill, darken on the shadow side, and pick up a rim light on the lit side. No black outlines.

Animate by the numbers

Built-in clips use hand-animation timing: one 75 ms frame, holds by repetition, squash and stretch about the feet, the head lagging two frames, a one-frame weapon smear.

Look, then iterate

Artists review zoomed grids and the at-scale scene, run cheap lints for palette, stray pixels and silhouette overlap, and file engine limits as requests. Five engine rounds came out of that loop.

Ship the pack

One command writes sprite sheets, GIFs, editable .aseprite sources, the palette as PNG and GIMP file, a lineup and a README with anchors, events and walk speeds.

Who did what

  • Engineer (the lead agent) built and owns the forge: renderer, shading passes, animation, VFX, lints, export.
  • Two artist agents worked headless, one per style. They designed each family over two rounds and filed every limit they hit.
  • A final art director, a fresh agent that built neither family, reviewed both packs frame by frame, rebuilt the weakest assets and signed off.

Hit / deathHit is 2 frames: recoil, then a flat off-white silhouette. Death is 14: pop up to 1.3× height, collapse, 1 px bounces, hold.
SizeCharacters 22–35 px tall, head about half the height, 8–25 colours per sprite.
RampsLightness rises about 0.1 per step, hue shifts about 15° per step toward yellow in the lights and violet in the shadows.
OutlinesNone. Most edge pixels keep their fill colour; the rest darken on the shadow side or catch rim light.
